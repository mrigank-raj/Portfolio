Case study

AI-First Cashless Claims

Cashless health claims stall at discharge, even when every document is in. I designed an AI layer that has the claim ready before the patient is.

Type
AI product case study
Scope
Health insurance, India
Stage
Concept, reviewed, pilot plan
Note
Independent, not affiliated with any insurer
Problem

Cashless claims stall at discharge, after the paperwork is done

Proposed

An AI layer that builds the claim from admission, so it's ready at discharge

Rejected

A policyholder-only app Shipping without a hospital

Decides it

Settled within 10 min of discharge, approval rates unchanged

01The brief

Design an AI-first product that simplifies the post-purchase insurance experience for Indian households: find the biggest problems after purchase, decide where AI genuinely helps, and pick one problem worth an MVP.

02What I found

  • Claims rejected or delayedUndisclosed pre-existing conditions, coverage gaps, missing documents and lapsed policies cause partial or full rejections.
  • Premium hikes at renewalSudden jumps, and steady increases with age, make a policy feel worse value every year.
  • Cashless claims delayed at dischargeBack and forth between hospital and insurer over the final bill, partial payouts, and last-minute rejections.
  • Renewal reminders that never arriveOutdated contact details lead to lapsed policies, and the changes at renewal are hard to understand.
  • Dark patterns on insurance platformsReal, but a pre-purchase problem, so I ruled it out of scope.

03The problem I picked

Cashless claims at discharge. The policyholder has done everything right and still waits, because the delay is a coordination failure between the hospital and the insurer that they have no control over: documents go back and forth, hospital tariffs get negotiated, and one pending report pauses everything.

The cost lands on the family. In my secondary research, 76% of policyholders take a loan or break an investment to cover the bill while they wait, and 3 in 5 now choose reimbursement over cashless just to avoid the delay.

Root causes: coordination failure, tariff disputes, document bottlenecks, no real-time escalation path, and fragmented infrastructure. Of these, coordination costs the most time. My working assumption: about 30 minutes per document check, which turns a one-hour job into six.

04What I'd build

An AI layer between hospitals and insurers (or their TPAs), built on NHCX, India's national health-claims exchange.

When a patient is admitted, it matches their phone number or Aadhaar on NHCX and checks whether they hold an eligible policy. If they do, it opens the claim straight away and reads every document and report as the hospital adds it, using standard tariffs so there's nothing to negotiate at the end. The insurer approves or rejects the case as it builds, not after discharge.

The family doesn't need to do anything. They see where the claim stands, and if it's rejected or only partly approved, they can escalate it to a person.

05What changed after review

Three questions from reviewers changed the plan.

Why would insurers adopt this, when slower payouts suit them? Because a failed cashless claim breaks trust. Even if the family pays out of pocket, they are unlikely to renew or buy another policy from that insurer.

What could ship in 8 weeks without hospital integration? An AI document checker for insurers that gives a go or no-go on what the customer uploads. Useful, but it leaves the slowest part, the hospital-to-insurer loop, untouched.

Which root cause matters most? Coordination. So the plan became a pilot with one hospital chain, because only the hospital can push documents for verification, and nothing gets paid until they're verified.

06What I rejected, and why

  • A policyholder-only appThe hospital holds the documents. An app on the family's phone can show the delay, but it can't remove it.
  • Shipping without a hospital partnerThe 8-week document checker is a fallback, not the product. The time is lost between hospital and insurer.
  • Solving all five problemsAn MVP solves one. Cashless delay hits families at their worst moment and has the clearest fix.

07How I'd measure it

Main metric target

10 min

from discharge papers being signed to the claim being settled, meaning payment has reached the hospital.

What moves it
  • Time from admission to claim case opened
  • Share of documents verified before discharge
  • Share of cases that need a person to step in
What must not get worse

Approval, partial and rejection rates stay at their baseline. If approvals jumped from 80% to 89% in two weeks, that's a red flag, not a win: the AI would be building cases too strongly, and one side loses either way.

08Pilot plan

  • Who: one multi-speciality hospital chain with high claim volume, health insurance only. Hospitals gain faster turnaround on payments, so I'd expect medium to high willingness.
  • What kills it: a major shift in approval, partial or rejection rates against the pre-pilot baseline.
  • What the family sees: almost nothing, by design. Status updates, and an escalation button if they need it.
