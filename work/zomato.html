Case study

Zomato

People open Zomato hungry, scroll, and close it without ordering.

Type
Teardown and feature proposal
Status
Proposal
Platform
iOS and Android
Note
Independent, not affiliated with Zomato
Problem

500 options, no signal, app closed without ordering

Proposed

Help me decide: three picks, each with a reason

Rejected

More filters Bigger discounts

Decides it

Browse → order conversion, order value held

01The problem

Users browse but don't order. With 500-plus options, everything starts to look the same, and nothing signals what's best right now. Scroll fatigue sets in, the app closes, and the order is lost.

This isn't a user failing to decide. It's a missing decision layer in the product.

How strong is this? My own experience and conversations with friends, who described the same two moments: everything looks good, or what they want is over budget.

02The proposal

A Help me decide button just below the search bar, where intent is highest. One tap returns three picks, each with a one-line reason: a safe choice from past orders, a best pick that's highly rated and fast right now, and something new that fits your history.

Picks are personalised to order history, budget and time of day.

03What I rejected, and why

  • More filtersFilters add choices to a problem caused by too many choices.
  • More discounts to tip the decisionIt moves the decision to price and teaches people to wait for deals.

04How I'd measure it

Main metric target

Browse → order

conversion for sessions that use Help me decide, compared with a control group.

What moves it
  • Share of hungry-but-undecided sessions that tap it
  • Time from opening the app to placing an order
  • Repeat use of the button within 30 days
What must not get worse

Average order value doesn't fall. Faster decisions shouldn't mean cheaper ones.
