What I built, and what I didn't

Six products, AI first. For each one: the obvious version I didn't build, what I built instead, and the number that tells me it's working.

LiveFrom a briefRAGEvals

HDFC Fund Facts

A RAG assistant for five HDFC mutual funds that cites a source for every answer and refuses anything that sounds like advice.

The obvious version

A finance chatbot that compares funds and calculates returns

A calculated return can't be traced to a source document, so nobody can check it. It answers in three sentences or fewer, links exactly one source, and declines anything that sounds like advice.

Also left outReturn calculationsFund comparisons

It's working if target

0

answers on the test set say something the source pages don't.

While catching over 95% of advice-seeking questions.

LiveLLMVisionHealth

BeyondLabel

An LLM that reads a food label and tells you what it does to your body, and what to eat instead.

The obvious version

An AI that simplifies the nutrition label

People weren't misreading labels. They thought muesli was healthy, or knew chips were bad without knowing how bad. So it explains the effect on your body, and suggests a swap for the same craving.

Also left outLive barcode scannerWhatsApp botCalorie logging

It's working if target

1 in 4

scans end with the person choosing the suggested swap.

Without letting a verdict take longer than 10 seconds.

From a briefAI agentMCP

App Review Pulse

An AI agent that turns hundreds of app-store reviews into a one-page weekly note, delivered through MCP.

The obvious version

A review dashboard with sentiment charts

Nobody opens a dashboard on a busy week. A 250-word note with the top three themes, three real quotes and three actions lands where the team already reads: Google Docs and Gmail.

Also left outMore than five themesReviewer names

It's working if target

1+

action from each weekly note makes it onto the backlog.

With no personal data in any note.

LiveExperimentationML

A/B Decision Engine

An experimentation engine with an uplift model that tells a team whether to ship, kill or keep testing, and whether to trust the result.

The obvious version

A calculator that checks if p is below 0.05

Real tests go wrong before significance matters: a broken traffic split, a team peeking daily, an average that hides who got hurt. It checks all three, then gives one verdict.

Also left outA dashboard before the maths was proven

What I measured measured

22.7% → 1%

false-positive rate from daily peeking, before and after sequential testing.

Stays under the 5% error rate it promises.

LiveFintechNo AI, on purpose

Plutus

A money app where we deliberately left AI out, so every number is arithmetic you can check.

Team project with Kaustubh Gaurav, product designer. I owned product.

The obvious version

A budgeting app with bank sync and an AI advisor

The problem was losing track of small daily spends and loans between friends. So logging takes seconds, every number is plain arithmetic, and money owed to you never looks like money you owe.

Also left outBank syncAI advisorForecastingAccounts

It's working if target

5 of 7

days in a new user's first week have at least one expense logged.

With no number on screen that can't be traced to a saved record.

LiveB2B SaaS

Kudos

Peer-to-peer recognition and rewards for teams.

The obvious version

An HR console where managers hand out rewards

When only managers can give recognition, they become the bottleneck and the people who see the work stay silent. So anyone can recognise anyone, on a ledger that can't lose a transfer halfway.

Also left outHR system integrationsReal gift-card payoutsNotifications

It's working if target

40%

of a team gives kudos every week by week six.

With zero points-balance mismatches.
